Method & Physical Modeling

        
Deterministic geophysical equations, multi-criteria hazard formulation, architectural flowchart, and transparent scientific disclosures.

      


      
      

        

           LunaSafe Autonomous End-to-End Geophysical Processing Pipeline
        

        
      


      
      

        
        

          
 Permanently Shadowed Region (PSR)

          

            Topographic depressions at lunar polar latitudes (> 80°S) where crater rims permanently occlude solar incidence over the 18.6-year lunar precession cycle. Ambient temperatures remain below 110 K, creating volatile cold traps for water ice. Optical guidance cannot observe landing terrain without active lidar.
          

        


        
        

          
 Topographic Slope (Horn 1981)

          

            Maximum rate of elevation change across an 8-neighbor moving kernel using central finite differences: 
            $\tan \theta = \sqrt{(\partial z / \partial x)^2 + (\partial z / \partial y)^2}$. Slopes < 5.0° ensure lander tip-over margins; slopes ≥ 15.0° cause catastrophic overturning for standard tripod lander chassis.
          

        


        
        

          
 Micro-Relief Roughness (σ)

          

            Standard deviation of detrended residual elevations within a local moving kernel ($\sigma_z = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum (z_i - \bar{z})^2}$). Represents surface obstacles, small craters, and boulder clusters that could penetrate engine bells or exceed leg clearance envelopes (> 1.5 m).
          

        


        
        

          
 Shadow Obscuration Fraction (Wd)

          

            Fractional illumination attenuation evaluated by grazing-angle ray casting against neighboring elevation barriers. Weight $W_d$ penalizes candidate landing sites with insufficient optical sensor visibility or solar power generation during surface operations.

 Transparent Physical Assumptions & Engineering Limitations

        

          

            1. Spatial Resolution Constraint: Orbital DEMs (240 m/px real or 20 m/px synthetic) cannot resolve boulders smaller than cell dimensions (< 1.0 m). Terminal obstacle clearance requires surface-relative hazard avoidance sensors (TRN/LiDAR).
          

          

            2. Instantaneous Illumination vs. Full Diurnal Cycle: Single-frame ray-tracing simulates instantaneous grazing illumination. Definitive volatile cold-trap analysis requires multi-year lunar precession tracking.
          

          

            3. Geotechnical Soil Bearing Capacity: Topographic laser altimetry measures surface relief, not soil shear strength or regolith compaction. Pad penetration depth cannot be inferred from DEMs alone.
          

          

            4. Radar / Active Sensor Requirement: Class 4 (Shadowed-Unknown) areas are not automatically dangerous slopes, but lack optical illumination. Landing in Class 4 strictly requires active surface illumination or RF guidance beacons.
